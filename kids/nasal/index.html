Understanding nasal congestion

Helping a child breathe

A practical guide for a 4-year-old with a blocked nose lasting more than 10 days, no fever, and mouth breathing that is worse at night.

Recommended next step: arrange a pediatrician visit

Because the blockage has lasted more than 10 days and is affecting breathing through the nose during sleep, arrange an examination within the next day or two. This is not necessarily an emergency if the child is otherwise breathing comfortably, active, and drinking normally.

What might be happening?

Three possibilities to discuss

These are possibilities, not a diagnosis. A clinician can examine the child and decide what is most likely.

01

Enlarged adenoids

Adenoids are tissue behind the nose. When enlarged, they can narrow the nasal airway.

  • Frequent mouth breathing
  • Snoring or restless sleep
  • Persistent nasal blockage
02

Allergic rhinitis

Dust mites, pollen, pets, or other triggers can inflame the lining of the nose.

  • Sneezing or itchy nose
  • Clear runny nose or congestion
  • Symptoms linked to bedroom or season
03

Ongoing sinus inflammation

A cold can leave nasal symptoms behind. Symptoms lasting over 10 days without improvement can sometimes suggest bacterial sinusitis.

  • Persistent congestion or discharge
  • Worsening after initial improvement
  • Sometimes cough or facial discomfort
Comfort measures

What you can safely try at home

These measures may ease symptoms while you arrange the medical review.

💧

Saline nasal spray or drops

Use plain saline according to the product directions, especially before bedtime. Encourage gentle nose blowing afterward.

🌫️

Clean cool-mist humidifier

Use a cool-mist humidifier if the room air is dry. Empty and clean it regularly. Avoid hot steam because of burn risk.

🥤

Offer fluids

Offer water regularly. Comfortable hydration can help keep mucus from becoming too thick.

🛏️

Make sleep comfortable

Try saline before bed and keep the bedroom smoke-free and comfortable. Use age-appropriate, safe sleeping arrangements.

Medication safety

What not to start on your own

  • Do not give over-the-counter cough and cold combination medicines unless the child's clinician specifically recommends them.
  • Do not use adult nasal decongestant sprays or start medicated nasal sprays without pediatric advice.
  • Do not use antibiotics unless a clinician diagnoses a condition that needs them.
  • If allergies are suspected, ask the pediatrician whether an age-appropriate allergy medicine or nasal treatment is suitable.
Know the warning signs

When to seek medical help

Emergency care now

  • Difficulty breathing, very rapid breathing, or skin pulling in around the ribs or neck
  • Bluish or gray lips or face
  • Severe sleepiness, difficulty waking, or signs of serious illness

Call emergency services if the child is struggling to breathe or has blue/gray lips or face.

Contact the pediatrician promptly

  • Snoring with pauses, gasping, or choking during sleep
  • Symptoms are worsening, or improve and then get worse
  • New fever, ear pain, facial pain, poor drinking, or reduced urination
  • One-sided blockage or foul-smelling discharge (a foreign object in the nose needs to be ruled out)

For the doctor

Three key questions to ask

Could enlarged adenoids be contributing to the blocked nose and mouth breathing?
Could this be allergic rhinitis or sinus inflammation?
Would an ENT (ear, nose and throat) assessment or sleep evaluation be appropriate?
Important: This page is general educational information, not a diagnosis or a substitute for care from a pediatrician. Nasal blockage lasting more than 10 days can have several causes; duration alone does not prove a bacterial infection. A clinician should assess the child and recommend treatment.
