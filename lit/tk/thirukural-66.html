குழலினிது யாழினிது என்பதம் மக்கள்
மழலைச்சொல் கேளா தவர்.

Kuzhal inidhu yaazhinidhu enbatham makkal
Mazhalaichchol kelaa thavar.

In essence

A child's innocent babbling is sweeter than the music of the flute or the lute— especially to a parent who has truly heard it.

A simple English rendering
“Those who have never heard their own child’s babbling may say that the flute is sweet and the lute is sweet.”

The Kural is not merely comparing two kinds of music. It celebrates a very human discovery: once we hear the spontaneous, imperfect sounds of a child we love, ordinary measures of beauty seem to change.

The heart of Kural 66

When love changes our definition of music

Classical instruments produce carefully shaped sounds. A baby’s speech is different: broken, playful, unpredictable and without musical rules. Yet to a loving parent, those little sounds can become more precious than the most refined melody.

A timeless observation

Why this Kural still feels modern

Beauty is personal

What sounds ordinary to one person can be priceless to another when memory, affection and relationship give it meaning.

Love creates attention

We listen differently when the sound belongs to someone we cherish. The Kural captures that transformation with remarkable simplicity.
