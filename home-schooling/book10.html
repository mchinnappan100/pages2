Literacy Review Activities

  1. Read a new picture book together. Ask all four comprehension levels: recall, sequence, infer, predict.
  2. Give 10 random word cards (mix of CVC, digraph, CVCE words). Ask the child to read each one independently.
  3. Ask the child to write a paragraph about their favorite thing they learned in the whole 10-book series.
  4. Read aloud the personal narrative and fiction story from Book 8. How has their writing grown?

Math Review Activities

  1. Play "Math Market" — set up a store with coins, let the child shop, pay, and receive change for 20 minutes.
  2. Write 5 word problems combining addition, subtraction, and multiplication. Solve with objects.
  3. Draw 5 two-digit numbers. Ask: "What are the tens? The ones? Which is largest?"
  4. Skip count by 2s, 3s, 4s, and 5s — all the way up.

5 School Readiness Conversations to Have Before Day One

  1. "School is a place where you will learn even MORE amazing things. Your teacher is another helper — like a community helper for your brain."
  2. "If you feel nervous, that's completely normal and it shows you care. Nervous feelings usually get smaller after the first day."
  3. "If something feels wrong or unfair, you can always tell your teacher, then tell me. You are never alone."
  4. "Not everyone will know as much as you do yet — that's okay. The kindest thing you can do is help, not show off."
  5. "Learning is something we do forever. School is just the next chapter. Every book you've read is still with you."

🌟 Unit 3 — Capstone Project: My Learning Story

🌟 The Big Project: My Learning Story

"My Learning Story" is a portfolio presentation — the child's opportunity to look back at everything they've done across 10 books and share it with a real audience (family members, grandparents, family friends).

This is not a test. It is a celebration. It is the child saying: "Look at what I can do. Look at who I've become."

What Goes in My Learning Story

📸

My Favorite Moment

One drawing or photo from each book — 10 images total, one highlight per book.

📖

A Book I Can Read

Choose one book (or passage) and read it aloud to the audience. Proof of reading.

✍️

My Best Writing

The personal narrative OR fiction story — read aloud as the "author."

🔢

A Math Challenge

Demonstrate one impressive math skill live — skip counting, solving a word problem, making change.

🔬

My STEAM Project

Present the documentation from one STEAM project — what I did, what I learned.

🎤

My Poem

Recite the "I Am" poem from memory — the beautiful finale of the presentation.

Presentation Day Preparation

  1. Invite an audience of 2–5 people (grandparents via video call count!). A real audience is essential.
  2. Practice the presentation 2–3 times before the day — familiarity builds confidence.
  3. Set up a special "stage" — a chair in front of a wall, with the child's artwork displayed around them.
  4. On the day: let the child introduce themselves and say: "I have been learning for [X months/weeks]. Today I want to show you what I know."
  5. After each section, the audience applauds. After the poem, they give a standing ovation.
❤️ Why this matters beyond the curriculum: A child who can stand before an audience, present their work with confidence, and take a bow for their accomplishments has developed the self-advocacy, public speaking, and self-efficacy that will serve them in every classroom, every job interview, and every relationship for the rest of their life. This presentation is not a school exercise. It is a life skill.

★Next Steps — The Adventure Continues

🌱 End of series — beginning of everything.

The 10-book series is complete. But learning never ends. Here is what's ahead:

  • Kindergarten or First Grade: Your child is ready — intellectually, socially, and emotionally. They will likely be one of the most prepared children in their class. Encourage them to be generous with their knowledge.
  • Continue reading aloud every single day — even when they can read independently. Read books a level or two above their reading level. This is the #1 thing you can do.
  • Follow curiosity — if they become obsessed with dinosaurs, space, trains, or baking, lean into it completely. Deep passion for one topic grows the neural architecture that accelerates learning in all topics.
  • Preserve the portfolio — store all artwork, writing, and project documentation from these 10 books. In 10 years, you will look at that body of work and be astonished. So will they.
  • Trust your instincts — you have proven through this series that you are an extraordinary educator. The skills you developed in these months are yours forever.
❤️ The final word: There is an ancient Chinese proverb: "The best time to plant a tree was 20 years ago. The second best time is now." You planted a tree. You watered it every week. You didn't give up when the seasons were difficult. And now — look at what is growing. It will stand for a lifetime. And someday, your child may plant a tree of their own. And they will know exactly how to do it — because they watched you.
