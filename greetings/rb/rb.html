What does it mean?

A thread that carries a promise.

Raksha Bandhan comes from two Sanskrit-derived words: raksha, meaning protection, and bandhan, meaning bond or tying.

Traditionally, a sister ties a rakhi around her brother’s wrist as a symbol of affection, trust and a wish for his well-being. The brother responds with love, a gift and a promise to stand by her.

Today, the festival has grown beyond a single ritual. It celebrates the many forms of siblinghood — biological siblings, cousins, chosen family, and people who have become family through love.

The ritual

A simple, meaningful sequence

1

Prepare the thali

A traditional plate may include a rakhi, roli or kumkum, rice, sweets, a diya and other family customs.

2

Offer blessings

A tilak may be placed on the forehead, followed by prayers or blessings for happiness, health and prosperity.

3

Tie the rakhi

The rakhi is tied around the wrist, expressing love, connection and a commitment to care for each other.

4

Share sweets & celebrate

End the ceremony with sweets, gifts, laughter, family photographs and — most importantly — time together.

Stories & tradition

Many stories. One enduring idea.

Raksha Bandhan is associated with a number of stories and regional traditions from Indian history and mythology. Their details vary, but many share a common theme: a sacred thread becomes a symbol of protection, loyalty, affection and mutual responsibility.

“The thread is small. The meaning is immense.”
