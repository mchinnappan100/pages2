A dedication to a master of simplicity · 1943—

Ken Thompson.

Small tools. Systems that last.

Programmer, co-creator of Unix and Turing Award laureate. Thompson built an operating system, a language, a text encoding and a chess machine. Much of what we run today still rests on that work.

01 / The journey

Eight decades of
building things small.

Thompson worked at the center of Bell Labs when Unix took shape, then carried the same habits into new systems, a new encoding and a new language.

1943

Born in New Orleans

Kenneth Lane Thompson is born on February 4.

1966

Bell Labs

After his engineering degrees at UC Berkeley, he joins Bell Labs and works on Multics.

1969

Unix begins

Writes the first version of Unix on a little-used PDP-7. The B language follows soon after.

1973

Unix in C

Thompson and Dennis Ritchie rewrite Unix in C. The grep tool appears in the same period.

1980

Belle wins

Belle, the chess machine he built with Joe Condon, wins the World Computer Chess Championship.

1983

Turing Award

Shares ACM's Turing Award with Dennis Ritchie for their work on Unix.

1992

UTF-8 and Plan 9

Designs UTF-8 with Rob Pike. It debuts in Plan 9, the Bell Labs successor to Unix.

2009

Go announced

Google reveals Go, co-designed by Thompson, Rob Pike and Robert Griesemer.

02 / The four ideas

Ideas that outlived
the machines.

The hardware Thompson started on is long gone. These habits of design are the part that stayed.

1

Simplicity

Unix began on a modest machine and stayed small enough for one person to understand.

2

Portability

Rewriting Unix in C let one system move between very different computers.

3

Universality

UTF-8 gives every writing system a place in one encoding, and plain ASCII stays unchanged.

4

Skepticism

In his Turing lecture he showed that a compiler can hide a flaw that reading the source will never reveal.

03 / The work

Systems, languages and
machines that changed computing.

Few people have shaped so many layers of the stack: the operating system, the languages above it, the text tools around it and the encoding beneath every page.

01 · OPERATING SYSTEM

Unix

Begun in 1969 at Bell Labs with Dennis Ritchie and colleagues. Its ideas live on in Linux, macOS and the BSDs.

02 · LANGUAGE

B

A compact language Thompson wrote for early Unix, derived from BCPL. Ritchie's C grew out of it.

03 · TEXT TOOLS

ed, regex and grep

Thompson brought regular expressions into everyday text editing. His ed editor led to the grep search tool.

04 · CHESS

Belle

Built with Joe Condon, Belle won the 1980 World Computer Chess Championship. Thompson also pioneered endgame tablebases.

05 · ENCODING

UTF-8 and Plan 9

With Rob Pike he designed UTF-8 in 1992 and helped build Plan 9 at Bell Labs.

06 · LANGUAGE

Go

Designed at Google with Rob Pike and Robert Griesemer. Announced in 2009, with version 1.0 following in 2012.

04 / One encoding

Every writing system.
One byte stream.

In 1992 Thompson and Rob Pike designed UTF-8 for Plan 9. Each Unicode character takes one to four bytes, and ASCII text stays exactly as it was. Pike has recalled sketching it on a diner placemat.

# code point  ->  UTF-8 bytes

A   U+0041  ->  41
é   U+00E9  ->  C3 A9
க   U+0B95  ->  E0 AE 95
語  U+8A9E  ->  E8 AA 9E

# ASCII is unchanged.
05 / The enduring legacy

You can't trust code that you did not totally create yourself.

A dedication inspired by Thompson's work: keep systems small enough to understand, build tools that outlast their machines, and question what you cannot inspect.
