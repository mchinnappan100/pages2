SCOTLAND · 1306–1307 · A STORY OF HOPE
01 / The struggle

A king who had almost lost everything.

More than seven hundred years ago, Scotland was fighting for its independence. Robert the Bruce had been crowned King of Scots in 1306.

But the road ahead was filled with danger. His forces suffered defeats. His enemies pursued him. His family and supporters faced terrible hardships.

Bruce was forced into hiding. The dream of a free Scotland seemed almost impossible. He wondered whether he should abandon the struggle.

"Was it time to give up, or was there still one more chance?"

According to the famous legend, it was in this moment of despair that something very small would teach a future king something enormous.

A KING WATCHES A TINY CREATURE
02 / The turning point

Six failures.
One more attempt.

Bruce looked up and noticed a spider trying to stretch its thread from one beam to another. It tried again and again. Each time, the thread failed to reach its destination.

The spider made another attempt. Bruce watched carefully. Would it fail again?

This time, the tiny creature succeeded. Its thread caught the opposite beam. The web could finally begin to take shape.

In the traditional telling, Bruce saw his own struggle reflected in the spider. If this little creature could try once more, why should he surrender?

"If at first you don't succeed, try, try again."

Inspired by the sight, Bruce resolved to continue his fight for Scotland.

03 / The road to freedom

A journey of courage.

The spider may belong to legend, but Robert the Bruce's struggle and eventual success belong to Scottish history.

1274

The birth of a future king

Robert Bruce was born on 11 July 1274.

1306

Crowned King of Scots

Bruce was crowned at Scone, beginning a difficult campaign to establish his authority.

1306–1307

Exile and determination

After early setbacks, Bruce survived in hiding and continued to rebuild his campaign.

1314

The Battle of Bannockburn

Bruce's forces achieved a major victory against the army of Edward II near Stirling.

1328

Recognition of Scottish independence

The Treaty of Edinburgh–Northampton formally recognised Scotland as an independent kingdom and Bruce as its king.

1329

The passing of a great king

Robert the Bruce died on 7 June 1329, remembered as one of Scotland's most significant monarchs.

02

Sometimes success is closer than we think. The next attempt may be the one that changes everything.
