A dedication to the designer of Pascal · 1934—2024

Niklaus Wirth.

Clear languages. Lean systems.

Computer scientist, designer of Pascal, Modula-2 and Oberon, and Turing Award laureate. Wirth held that good software should be simple enough to understand, and he built whole languages and systems to show it could be done.

01 / The journey

A career spent making
software smaller.

Wirth moved between Switzerland and California, but his work kept one direction: languages and systems that a person can hold in their head.

1934

Born in Winterthur

Niklaus Wirth is born in Switzerland on February 15.

1963

Berkeley and Stanford

Earns his PhD at UC Berkeley, then teaches at Stanford.

1966

ALGOL W

Builds ALGOL W, a refinement of ideas from C. A. R. Hoare that points toward Pascal.

1970

Pascal

At ETH Zurich, where he became professor in 1968, his team produces the first Pascal compiler.

1976

The textbook

Publishes Algorithms + Data Structures = Programs, a book whose title became a creed.

1984

Turing Award

ACM honors him for developing a sequence of innovative computer languages.

1987

Oberon

With Jürg Gutknecht, he completes Oberon: a compact language and operating system.

2024

Farewell

Dies on January 1, aged 89.

02 / The four ideas

How to build software
you can understand.

Wirth's languages differ, but a few convictions repeat in all of them.

1

Clarity

Pascal was designed so students could learn structured programming and see what their code meant.

2

Stepwise refinement

His 1971 paper taught programmers to start with a broad idea and refine it into detail, one decision at a time.

3

Leanness

In "A Plea for Lean Software" (1995) he argued that bloat outruns hardware gains. The observation is now called Wirth's law.

4

The whole stack

His group designed language, compiler, operating system and even hardware together, so each part fit the others.

03 / The work

Languages, a workstation
and a very small system.

Few computer scientists designed so many complete pieces and then taught them in print.

01 · LANGUAGE

Pascal

Designed in the late 1960s and first compiled in 1970. Built to teach structured programming, and named for Blaise Pascal.

02 · EARLY LANGUAGES

Euler and ALGOL W

Earlier experiments in a cleaner ALGOL family. They shaped the design that became Pascal.

03 · LANGUAGES

Modula-2 and Oberon

Modula-2 (1978) added modules for large systems. Oberon (1987) pared the ideas back to essentials.

04 · HARDWARE

Lilith

A personal workstation built by his ETH group around 1980, designed to run Modula-2.

05 · SYSTEM

Oberon system

With Jürg Gutknecht, a full operating system and compiler small enough to be described in a book, Project Oberon.

06 · BOOK

Algorithms + Data Structures = Programs

A 1976 textbook that taught a generation to see programs as algorithms joined to data.

04 / Readable by design

Code that reads like
a clear argument.

Pascal asks you to declare what you use, structure your control flow and let the compiler check your types. Many programmers met those habits in a Pascal course.

{ Pascal, in four lines }

program Hello(output);
begin
  writeln('Hello, world')
end.
05 / The enduring legacy

Algorithms + Data Structures = Programs.

A dedication inspired by Wirth's work: make it simple, make it clear, and be wary of anything that grows without need.
