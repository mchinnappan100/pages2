A Humble Dedication

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi

Mahatma  ·  Bapu  ·  Father of the Nation

✦   ✦   ✦

To the man who taught the world that the mightiest weapon is not the sword, but the human soul armed with truth and love — we bow in eternal gratitude.

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, whom his people called Bapu — Father — and the poet Rabindranath Tagore crowned Mahatma — Great Soul — walked barefoot across a continent's conscience and moved it. He showed that ordinary people, armed with extraordinary courage, can bend the arc of history toward justice.

This page is offered in reverence to his memory, his teaching, and the undying flame of Ahimsa he carried from the villages of India to the conscience of all humanity.

◈ Pillars of His Philosophy

☮

Ahimsa

Non-violence in thought, word, and deed — the supreme ethical principle and foundation of all his campaigns.

✦

Satyagraha

Truth-force or soul-force: the discipline of non-violent resistance, anchored in love rather than hatred for the oppressor.

⚖

Swaraj

Self-rule — both political independence from colonial power and inner self-mastery of the individual soul.

🌾

Sarvodaya

The welfare of all: a vision of a society built not on competition but on mutual service and compassion.

🧵

Swadeshi

Economic self-reliance through locally produced goods — symbolised by the spinning wheel he made an icon of resistance.

🙏

Brahmacharya

Celibacy and self-discipline: the cultivation of inner strength needed to sustain a life of fearless service.

◈ Milestones of a Great Life

1869

Birth in Porbandar

Born on 2 October into a devout Hindu family in the coastal town of Porbandar, Gujarat.

1888

Journey to London

Sailed to England to study law at the Inner Temple, graduating as a barrister in 1891.

1893

South Africa & Awakening

Thrown off a first-class train in Pietermaritzburg for being non-white — an incident that transformed his life's mission.

1906

Birth of Satyagraha

Launched his first mass non-violent resistance campaign in South Africa against the Transvaal Asiatic Registration Act.

1915

Return to India

Returned home and established the Sabarmati Ashram, beginning his immersion in the lives of India's poor.

1919

Non-Cooperation Movement

Led nationwide protests against the Rowlatt Act and the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, forging a unified mass movement.

1930

The Salt March

Led the historic 241-mile Dandi March to protest the British salt tax — a defining act of civil disobedience that echoed worldwide.

1942

Quit India Movement

Issued the clarion call "Do or Die" — demanding an immediate end to British rule and uniting 300 million Indians.

1947

Independence of India

India achieved independence on 15 August. Gandhi mourned the partition while walking among riot-torn villages to restore peace.

1948

Martyrdom

Assassinated on 30 January at Birla House, New Delhi. His last words were "Hey Ram." The world fell silent.

◈ An Immortal Legacy

Mahatma Gandhi proved that a single unarmed individual, sustained by moral courage and rooted in love, can challenge the greatest empires and win. His methods of non-violent civil disobedience became the template for liberation movements across the twentieth century — from the American civil rights marches led by Martin Luther King Jr. to the anti-apartheid struggle of Nelson Mandela in South Africa.

The United Nations declared 2 October — his birthday — the International Day of Non-Violence, a date observed in every country on earth as a reminder that peace is not passivity but the highest form of human courage.

His spinning wheel graces the Indian national flag. His face adorns every denomination of the Indian rupee. His statue stands in the squares of London, Washington D.C., Johannesburg, and Geneva. Yet his truest monument is not made of stone: it is every act of peaceful resistance, every moment a human being chooses conscience over convenience.

"My life is my message." He lived those four words completely — and that message still speaks.
