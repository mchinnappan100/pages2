Why reuse it?

Aluminum makes a surprisingly good garden material

Aluminum siding is lightweight and naturally resistant to rust. With proper framing, it can become an excellent exterior skin for raised beds and garden structures.

🪶

Lightweight

Easier to move, cut and install than many heavier metal materials.

🛡️

Corrosion Resistant

Aluminum does not rust like ordinary steel, making it useful outdoors.

Reuse & Recycle

⚠️ Important structural principle

Do not depend on thin aluminum siding alone to hold a deep bed of wet soil. Soil creates significant outward pressure. Use a wood or metal frame to provide structural support and attach the aluminum siding to that frame.

🔨 Basic Construction Method

  1. 1

    Build the frame

    Construct a rectangular frame from suitable outdoor-rated lumber or metal framing.

  2. 2

    Prepare the aluminum

    Measure and cut the siding panels to fit the outside walls. Carefully deburr sharp edges.

  3. 3

    Attach the siding

    Fasten the aluminum to the frame using corrosion-resistant screws or appropriate exterior fasteners.

  4. 4

    Prepare the bottom

    Place the bed on suitable ground. Preserve drainage and avoid creating a waterlogged container.

  5. 5

    Fill with soil

    Add a suitable garden soil/compost mixture and start planting.

🦺 Safety First

Protect yourself

Cut aluminum can leave extremely sharp edges. Wear gloves and eye protection when cutting and drilling.

Remove sharp edges

Deburr cut edges and cover exposed edges where people might contact them.

Know your material

If the siding is old, determine whether it has coatings, paint or other materials that could be unsuitable around edible crops.

Support the soil

Always provide adequate structural support. A beautiful aluminum wall is not useful if it bows outward under soil pressure.
